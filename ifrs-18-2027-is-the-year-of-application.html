IFRS 18: 2027 Is the Year of Application, Not Implementation

The effective date is 1 January 2027. But companies that wait until 2027 to start implementation will already be late.
By Goran Lazarević · 11 September 2026

IFRS 18 is often discussed as a 2027 reporting change. Formally, that is correct. Operationally, it is the wrong way to think about it.

IFRS 18 Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements replaces IAS 1 and is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027. The Standard introduces a more structured statement of profit or loss, new defined subtotals, requirements for management-defined performance measures and enhanced principles for aggregation and disaggregation.

My view is simple:
2027 should be the year in which the new reporting model is applied. The implementation work should largely be completed during 2026.

Why 2026 already matters

There are three reasons why finance teams should treat 2026 as the implementation year rather than the waiting year.

1. IAS 8 already brings IFRS 18 into the 2026 financial statements

When a new IFRS Accounting Standard has been issued but is not yet effective, IAS 8 requires an entity to disclose that fact and provide known or reasonably estimable information relevant to assessing the possible impact of the new Standard in the period of initial application.

That means a generic sentence saying that “management is assessing the impact” may no longer be enough if the organisation already has information that can reasonably explain where the impact is expected to arise.

By 31 December 2026, a mature implementation project should therefore be able to explain, at least qualitatively and where practicable quantitatively, the major areas affected by IFRS 18.

2. The 2026 comparative period will be presented under IFRS 18

IFRS 18 requires retrospective application. In the first annual financial statements applying the Standard, the immediately preceding comparative period must be restated, and a reconciliation is required for each line item in the statement of profit or loss between the restated IFRS 18 amounts and the amounts previously presented under IAS 1.

For a calendar-year company applying IFRS 18 from 1 January 2027, that means the 2026 profit or loss information will need to be available in the new structure.

This is not merely a disclosure exercise at the end of 2027. It has implications for mapping, chart-of-accounts logic, reporting packages, consolidation systems, management reporting and the way comparative information is retained during 2026.

3. Q1 2027 is much closer than the 2027 annual report

Companies publishing interim financial statements under IAS 34 cannot plan implementation around the 2027 year-end close. IFRS 18 contains specific transition requirements for interim financial reporting in the first year of application, including presentation of the required headings and subtotals and reconciliations for comparative profit or loss information.

For listed groups and other entities with quarterly or half-year reporting, the real operational deadline is therefore the first 2027 reporting cycle—not December 2027.

What is actually changing?

IFRS 18 is sometimes described as a “presentation standard”. That description is technically understandable but operationally misleading. Presentation is the visible output. The work underneath it reaches into reporting methodology, data, systems, management communication and controls.

For most non-financial corporates, implementation will require detailed work in at least four areas:

  1. Profit or loss structure. Income and expenses need to be classified into operating, investing and financing categories, alongside income taxes and discontinued operations, with new defined subtotals including operating profit and profit before financing and income taxes.
  2. Management-defined performance measures (MPMs). Measures used in public communications need to be inventoried and assessed to determine whether they meet the IFRS 18 definition and therefore require a dedicated note, reconciliation and related disclosures.
  3. Aggregation and disaggregation. Companies need to reassess whether information is grouped or split in a way that faithfully communicates material information, including how operating expenses are presented and disclosed.
  4. Systems and reporting architecture. Existing mappings, reporting packages, consolidation logic, chart-of-accounts structures and management reports need to be tested against the new presentation model.

The issue is bigger than the statutory financial statements

A key implementation risk is inconsistency between the IFRS financial statements and the way management communicates performance elsewhere.

Investor presentations, annual reports, press releases, management commentary, KPI decks and other public communications may contain subtotals that management uses to explain the company’s performance. Under IFRS 18, some of those measures may become management-defined performance measures and therefore enter the audited financial statements through specific disclosure requirements.

This creates a direct link between financial reporting, investor relations, controlling, corporate communications and audit. IFRS 18 implementation should therefore not be owned by accounting in isolation.

A practical 2026 readiness checklist

  • Define the target IFRS 18 statement of profit or loss.
  • Map existing accounts and reporting lines to the new categories and subtotals.
  • Assess whether the entity has specified main business activities that affect classification.
  • Inventory performance measures used in public communications and identify potential MPMs.
  • Review aggregation, disaggregation and operating-expense presentation.
  • Prepare the 2026 comparative mapping and determine how reconciliation data will be retained.
  • Assess required changes to ERP, consolidation, reporting and management-information systems.
  • Prepare the IAS 8 disclosure for the 31 December 2026 financial statements based on the implementation status and reasonably estimable impact.
  • Align finance, controlling, investor relations, communications and external audit before the first 2027 reporting cycle.

The most expensive option is to treat IFRS 18 as a year-end formatting exercise

If implementation starts only when the first 2027 financial statements are being prepared, the finance team will be trying to solve classification, data, systems, comparative information, MPMs and audit questions at the same time as the reporting close.

The better approach is to use 2026 to test the future presentation, run parallel mappings, challenge management measures, identify data gaps and agree the accounting methodology before the first mandatory reporting period starts.

2027 is the year of application.
2026 is the year of implementation.

That distinction may determine whether IFRS 18 becomes a controlled reporting transition—or another last-minute finance project.
